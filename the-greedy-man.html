The Greedy Man

A fictional story about kindness, compassion, and learning to change.

A story in scenes

Scene 1 of 7

One afternoon, wealthy Calvin sat in his luxurious car when he saw a woman asking for help.

The woman

“Could you spare a dollar? I haven’t had anything to eat today.”

Calvin

“A dirty, poor woman like you? Never!” He spat near her and drove away.

The woman

“You don’t know what you’ve done,” she said softly.

That night, Calvin came home tired and fell asleep on the couch. The next morning, he hurried to look in the mirror.

Calvin

“Who is that? This can’t be me!”

Narrator

His reflection showed an old man in worn clothes. Calvin had become someone who needed help himself.

Later, Calvin saw the same woman in a car. She stopped and offered him a dollar.

Calvin

“Please, could you help me with a dollar?”

The woman

“Here you are. Be wise with what you’ve learned.”

Narrator

Calvin watched her drive away and held the dollar carefully in his hand.

For the first time, Calvin understood how lonely and frightening it could feel to need help.

Calvin

“Why was I so greedy? Why did I treat people as if they didn’t matter?”

Narrator

He knew he had to face the people he had hurt and try to make things right.

Calvin began finding the people he had treated badly and apologized to each one.

Calvin

“I’m sorry for the way I treated you. You deserved kindness, and I was wrong.”

Narrator

Some people needed time. Calvin listened, respected their feelings, and kept trying to do better.

After two days of making amends, Calvin was exhausted. That night, he lay on the ground beneath the stars and fell asleep.

Calvin

“I’ll remember what it feels like to need help. I want to be kinder from now on.”

Narrator

When morning came, Calvin opened his eyes, back in his own body again.

Calvin thanked God for another chance and chose to use his wealth to help feed people in need.

Calvin

“A person’s worth isn’t measured by how much money they have. Everyone deserves compassion.”

The lesson Real change begins when we recognize the hurt we’ve caused and choose to treat others with kindness.
